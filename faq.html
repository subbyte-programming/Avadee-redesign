A FEW THINGS YOU MAY BE WONDERING

Questions about therapy.

Starting therapy can bring up questions. Here are answers to some common ones. You can also get in touch if you would like to ask something else.

Does going to therapy mean I am weak?

No. Reaching out for support takes awareness and courage. Therapy can help you identify strengths you have used before and consider how to draw on them in what is happening now.

How is therapy different from talking with a friend or family member?

A therapist brings professional training and experience, offers a nonjudgmental place to talk, and can help you explore new skills and perspectives. Therapy is confidential, with legal and safety-related exceptions.

Why not take medication instead?

Medication can be effective and may be used alongside therapy. Therapy offers a place to explore underlying concerns and develop strategies for personal or relationship goals. Discuss medication decisions with a qualified medical professional.

What happens in a therapy session?

Each person's concerns and goals are different, so the approach is tailored to your needs. You and your therapist can talk about what brings you in and shape the work together.

How long does therapy take?

There is no general timeline that fits everyone. The length of therapy depends on your circumstances, goals, participation, and the concerns bringing you to therapy.

How can I get the most out of therapy?

Active participation and commitment matter. The work you choose to do between sessions can support the progress you make together during appointments.
